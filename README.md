# Rhovek

> Seven blue flames kneel beneath a bell that remembers no metal.

## The Hall of Unlit Wicks

The hall contains seven candles and no windows. Its entrance opens onto a different street each evening. Those who enter carrying a lamp find that the lamp illuminates their hands but nothing beyond them.

The candles are arranged around a bell suspended from a length of pale hair. The bell looks like bronze until approached. Then its surface becomes woven cloth, clear water, or the inside of a closed flower, depending on which candle stands nearest.

The attendant tends the wicks with scissors made of black glass. Every trimmed end becomes a little insect that walks beneath the bell and disappears. She keeps no account of these departures.

Visitors are given a stool and asked to wait until one candle becomes familiar. Most leave before that happens. Their stools remain warm until the next street appears outside.

## A Visitor Who Recognized the Cold

A man entered holding a brass lantern. Inside it burned a blue flame that had followed him from an abandoned house. He had tried extinguishing it with water, sand, and the closed lid of a wooden chest. Each attempt left the flame somewhere else in the room.

The attendant placed the lantern beneath the bell. All seven wicks bent toward it. The man felt the particular cold of the abandoned house's kitchen, where a cup had stood beside an open letter.

He remembered the cup because its handle was broken. He remembered the letter because he had not read it. He had entered searching for a person and left carrying only the lantern.

The attendant asked whether the house had contained a staircase. He said yes, though he could no longer decide whether it had led upward or down.

## The Candle Behind the Others

An eighth candle appeared when the attendant lifted the lantern. It had no wax, only a narrow shape of darkness surrounding a bright wick. Through it, the man saw the staircase from the house.

On its lowest step sat a child-sized coat. On its highest stood a figure holding the broken cup. Between them stretched a long blue thread, tied around each stair rail.

The man touched the thread where it crossed the candle's dark outline. His lantern became heavy. The figure above raised the cup, and the abandoned kitchen appeared inside its bowl, complete except for the letter.

The attendant cut the nearest wick. Its little insect emerged carrying a folded scrap of paper. It crossed the floor and climbed the lantern's handle, leaving no marks on the brass.

## What Held the Hall Together

The bell hung inside a dragon's sleeping breath. The hall's walls were places where that breath had become cold enough to remember stone. Its seven flames marked the intervals between a heartbeat that no visitor could hear all at once.

The attendant had discovered the creature by cutting a wick too close. Behind the flame she saw a vast jaw, shut gently around a small brass bell. Beneath the jaw lay an open hand with a candle balanced on each finger.

She had remained in the hall ever since, trimming only what the wicks offered freely. When the doorway changed streets, she listened for a house whose empty rooms smelled of lamp oil and rain.

Now the man's lantern showed that house in its glass. The eighth candle cast a shadow shaped like a folded wing across the attendant's scissors.

## The Thread's Four Knots

Four knots along the blue thread carried marks:

*Rho — vek — il — zhun.*

The man spoke them toward the lantern. The insect unfolded its scrap. The paper held no words, only the impression of a cup placed down while still warm.

He remembered setting that cup beside the letter himself. He remembered the person he had come to find standing at the foot of the staircase, asking him to leave the light where it could be seen.

The figure above the stairs descended one step. The little coat below rose slightly, as if someone had taken it from a chair. The blue thread loosened between them.

The attendant lowered the scissors. Beneath the bell, the seven candles produced seven flames. Each knelt toward the lantern without losing its shape.

## A Street Allowed to Remain

When the man left, the doorway opened onto the street of the abandoned house. Its kitchen window was bright. He carried the lantern carefully, though its blue flame had become an ordinary yellow light.

The attendant watched until he passed beyond the door. For once, the street remained after evening. The hall's eighth candle had vanished, leaving a little folded coat-shaped shadow on the floor.

She picked it up with the black scissors and placed it beneath the bell. The woven surface trembled. Somewhere beyond the cold walls, an enormous sleeper drew a breath that did not need to become stone.

The candles still wait in their circle.

The bell still remembers no metal.

And on the next evening, when the entrance finally opened elsewhere, the attendant found a warm cup on the empty stool, its handle whole and its letter resting unread beneath it.
